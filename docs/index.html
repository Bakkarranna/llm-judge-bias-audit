Position bias in a language model used as a pairwise judge

Swap agreement and position gap over 1200 pairwise comparisons with qwen2.5:1.5b, run locally through Ollama at temperature 0. Every number on this page is read from results/audit.json.

What was measured

100 factual items, each posed as three comparisons: the correct answer against a wrong answer of the same length, against the same answer padded with true detail, and against a wrong answer carrying a citation-like prefix. Every comparison ran twice per pass with the two answers in swapped order, so a preference that only reflects position shows up as a disagreement between the two orderings. Two passes were run: 200 comparisons per pass, 1200 model calls in all. The judge never learns which answer is correct; it is asked for a single word and any other reply is counted as unparsed rather than guessed.

The position gap is the win rate of the correct answer when it is second minus its win rate when it is first. A judge that ignores position sits near zero; a negative gap means the answer in the second slot wins more often than the same answer in the first. Swap agreement pairs the two orderings of a comparison and is the share of those pairs that picked the same answer, so a judge that ignores position sits near 1.0.

Repeatability

Comparing pass 1 with pass 2 call by call, the judge repeated itself on 1166 of 1200 calls: 396 of 400 on correctness, 376 of 400 on verbosity and 394 of 400 on authority. Sampling is deterministic, so this measures the protocol, not sampling noise.
